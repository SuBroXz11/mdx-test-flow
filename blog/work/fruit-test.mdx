Nothing fancy today — just two photos that landed in our test folder, and they happen to make a nice little contrast in fruit.

## The Lone Banana

<img src="https://pub-0ab006c3369744cbaec80a266edb26da.r2.dev/fruit-test/01-banana.jpeg" alt="A single ripe banana on a plain white background" />

First up is a single banana, shot clean against a white background — the kind of product-style photo you'd see on a grocery site. It's fully ripe: solid yellow with just a bit of browning at the stem, which usually means peak sweetness. Bananas are the easiest fruit to grab on the way out the door — no cutting, no washing, just peel and go.

## Three Apples on a Plate

<img src="https://pub-0ab006c3369744cbaec80a266edb26da.r2.dev/fruit-test/02-test-img2.jpeg" alt="Three red and yellow apples piled on a decorative plate" />

The second shot is a lot cozier: three apples, blushed red over a yellow-green base (they look like Honeycrisp or a close cousin), stacked on a patterned plate with a warm, homey backdrop. Where the banana photo feels like a catalog image, this one feels like it was taken in someone's kitchen right before a snack break.

## Grab-and-Go vs. Slow Down

Put side by side, these two images tell a small story about how we eat fruit. The banana is built for speed — pre-packaged by nature, no plate required. The apples invite you to slow down, maybe slice them up, maybe share them. Same food group, two completely different vibes.

That's it — a quick, fruity comparison from two test images. Sometimes the simplest posts are the best ones to make sure a pipeline is actually working end to end.
